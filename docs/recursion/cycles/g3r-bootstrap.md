# G3R bootstrap recursive inquiry

## Anchor and limitation

The cycle begins at clean protected-main anchor
`4ba8e85a167affae79089da215f2b78565a38f38`. The sealed predecessor can ratchet retained
representations and admit a fixed learned-probe shape, but it cannot preserve, compare,
and act on project-level question, method, Goal, or implementation successors.

The consequential boundary is whether RCI can derive a checked next capability from a
demonstrated limitation. Without that bridge, two roadmap successors with different
future consequences are chosen by transient developer judgment and the project cannot
replay why one became next.

## How this changes the development agent

G3R does not make Codex intrinsically more knowledgeable or trustworthy. It externalizes
failure modes that otherwise remain hidden in one transient context:

- context loss becomes an exact anchor, immutable Goal, checkpoint, and handoff;
- self-confirmation becomes separate expected return, captured tool evidence, fresh
  exact-head review, and hosted CI;
- roadmap inertia becomes a typed successor frontier rather than automatic sequencing;
- category collapse is blocked by distinct theory, question, probe, representation,
  method, Goal, and implementation successor kinds;
- acceptance drift is blocked by a Goal and gate digest sealed before implementation;
- over-editing is bounded by allowed mutation roots, protected capabilities, rollback,
  and reopening;
- false continuity is replaced by append-only cycle records and explicit `Unknown` or
  stop dispositions.

The agent remains capable of making errors. The benefit is that its claims about its own
improvement must be exposed to mechanisms the candidate does not control: compiler and
type-checker output, adversarial/property tests, independently preserved research,
distinct-context review, and protected CI.
